# Indicator Selection

## 1. Purpose

This document operationalizes the conceptual framework defined in the project design. It reviews commonly used indicators in established international climate-risk and adaptation frameworks and identifies a candidate set of state-level indicators for Mexico.

The indicator-selection process is guided by four principles:

- Conceptual relevance: Each indicator must clearly represent Hazard, Exposure, Sensitivity or Adaptive Capacity.
- Avoidance of duplication: Indicators that measure substantially overlapping concepts should not be included together.
- Subnational applicability: Indicators must be meaningful for comparisons across Mexico’s 32 federal entities.
- Data feasibility: Data should ideally be available for all states from reliable and publicly accessible sources.

## 2. Reference Frameworks

The IPCC conceptualizes climate risk through interactions among climate-related hazards, the exposure of people and assets, and the vulnerability of human and natural systems. The IPCC provides the conceptual basis for the framework but does not prescribe one mandatory set of indicators for all assessments.

ND-GAIN

Examples include projected climate changes, rural population, water withdrawals, urban concentration, access to drinking water, sanitation, medical resources, electricity and infrastructure quality.

INFORM Risk Index

INFORM emphasizes physical exposure to specific hazards, socioeconomic conditions, vulnerable population groups, institutional capacity and infrastructure. Its methodology recommends selecting indicators that are relevant, representative, transparent, conceptually clear, reliable, open and scalable to subnational analysis.

## 3. Findings by Dimension

### 3.1 Hazard

Indicators commonly used

The literature commonly represents climate hazards through indicators describing the frequency, intensity or occurrence of climate-related events. Frequently used measures include droughts, floods, extreme heat, water stress, tropical cyclones and wildfires.

Assessment

Given the Mexican context and the objective of this project, priority will be given to hazards that are relevant across most states and for which consistent state-level data are available.

Potential Indicators

- Drought frequency or intensity
- Flood occurrence or susceptibility
- Extreme heat frequency or temperature anomaly
- Water stress or renewable water availability

### 3.2 Exposure

Indicators commonly used

Exposure is typically measured as the presence of people, infrastructure, productive assets or ecosystems located in areas affected by climate hazards. International frameworks increasingly favor hazard-specific exposure indicators over general demographic measures.

Assessment

Whenever possible, the framework will prioritize indicators that directly represent populations or assets exposed to climate hazards. General proxies such as population density will only be considered if more specific indicators are unavailable.

Potential Indicators

- Population exposed to climate hazards
- Cropland or agricultural assets exposed to drought
- Built-up area or infrastructure exposed to climate hazards
- Population density (proxy indicator)

### 3.3 Sensitivity

Indicators commonly used

Sensitivity is commonly represented by socioeconomic conditions that increase the susceptibility of communities to climate impacts. Frequently used indicators include poverty, dependence on climate-sensitive livelihoods, rural population and social marginalization.

Assessment

The selected indicators should capture structural socioeconomic conditions that influence how severely climate hazards affect different populations while minimizing redundancy between variables.

Potential Indicators

- Multidimensional poverty rate
- Agricultural employment
- Rural population
- Marginalization Index
- Income inequality

### 3.4 Adaptive Capacity

Indicators commonly used

Adaptive Capacity captures the socioeconomic and infrastructural conditions that enable populations to prepare for, respond to, and recover from climate-related hazards. It is measured through indicators of education, household water infrastructure, drainage infrastructure, and healthcare access.

Assessment

Priority will be given to indicators representing human capital, essential infrastructure and access to information, as these are consistently identified in international climate adaptation frameworks and are generally available at the state level.

- Potential Indicators
- Educational attainment
- Access to drinking water
- Access to sanitation
- Internet access
- Healthcare availability
- Disaster preparedness or institutional capacity

### Methodological Notes

- Hazard indicators were obtained from the **CENAPRED National Risk Atlas**, ensuring methodological consistency across all hazard variables.
- Municipal hazard classifications (Very Low–Very High) were converted to a numerical scale (1–5) and aggregated to the state level using **population-weighted averages** based on municipal population.
- The final framework contains **12 indicators** distributed across the four dimensions of the climate resilience framework:
  - **Hazard:** 3 indicators
  - **Exposure:** 2 indicators
  - **Sensitivity:** 3 indicators
  - **Adaptive Capacity:** 4 indicators
- All indicators were harmonized at the state level to ensure comparability across Mexico's 32 federal entities.
